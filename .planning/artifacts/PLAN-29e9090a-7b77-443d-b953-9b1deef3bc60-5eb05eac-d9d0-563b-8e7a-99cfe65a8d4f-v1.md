## Dependency rules

- P1 foundation work is independent of product-source build or installation.
- P2 and P3 may prepare source and governance without satisfying installed activation.
- P4 is independently owned and cannot be bypassed, shortened, or replaced by local product changes.
- P5 must satisfy P4’s exact external interfaces before P6–P8 can claim installed readiness.
- P6 and P7 may use separate worktrees only where lifecycle/lock authority does not overlap; overlapping lifecycle mutations remain serialized.
- P8 requires exact merged inputs from P3, P5, P6, and P7 plus the P4 external chain.
- P9 may perform partial authority and source-installer cutover before live migration; only its final compatibility-retirement step requires verified migration/update evidence and the full retention window.

## Completion semantics

- Source extraction complete does not mean repository governance complete.
- Repository governance complete does not mean CI operational.
- CI operational does not mean a signed release exists.
- Signed release does not mean clean installation, migration, or update passed.
- Successful update does not mean channel promotion or legacy retirement.
- None of the above means UAT accepted.

An observed result is evidence only. Phase status changes require the governed repository’s validated transition and exact supporting evidence.

## Requirement coverage and execution gates

P1–P2 and P9 cover EX-01–04; P3 covers GV-01–05; P4–P5 cover SF-01–07; P6 and P9 cover MG-01–04 and MG-06–08; P7–P8 cover MG-05 and RL-01–06. Safety, cancellation, concurrency, and fixture isolation remain regression gates in every source-changing phase. Each phase requires its exact merged revision, applicable required checks, retained test receipts, and owner-bound evidence before completion can be recorded. No phase may fabricate an external release, elapsed compatibility window, native result, or operator acceptance.

The accepted estimate is 26–42 engineering days, excluding external controller/AWA waiting, credential administration, and operator UAT. It assumes reuse of the existing runtime/test architecture with one application engineer and release-engineering support. Parallel work requires independent files and working trees; one writer owns overlapping lifecycle and lock code.
