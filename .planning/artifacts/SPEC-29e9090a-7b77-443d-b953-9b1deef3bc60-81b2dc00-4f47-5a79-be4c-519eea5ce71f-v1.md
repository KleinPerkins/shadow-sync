# Production child ownership and bounded cleanup correction

## Authority, scope and evidence

This specification implements approved PRD v3 REQ-SF-05 and PLAN v3 P3-CI-01/P6. REQ-GV-06/07/08/11 govern registration, exact evidence and documentation; REQ-MG-03/06 retain migration and single-operation-authority gates. Process ownership belongs to SF-05, not SF-04 (the external matcher contract). Correct that reference in future observations without changing frozen predecessor bytes.

An independent source audit found three concrete signaling/reaping interleavings: WorkerSession can signal while WorkerClient reaps; CancellationToken releases its lock before sending while DescriptorProcessRunner or ClosedProcessRunner reaps; post-worker-loss cleanup signals a recorded child whose reaper is outside the app. It also found no terminal post-KILL deadline in the two runners and the GitHub adapter, and Foundation Process callers that do not establish the process-group precondition used by their escalation helper. These are source-supported defects, not an observed live PID-reuse incident. The exact private audit input manifest is SHA-256 `b47dd7fe57ef236499b9b32d0a2c4a3145fa6ad51563bc8b345800559ff49ebe`.

DiscoveryGitProbe and the GitHub adapter serialize signals with their own direct-child waits on one thread; an identity-check/syscall gap alone does not prove a PID-reuse race there. Preserve those distinctions. The testing-only native fixture already serializes its own waits and signals, but that seam does not qualify production ownership.

## Direct-child owner contract

Add one core lifecycle owner for a process returned by a caller's successful posix_spawn. Adoption is permitted only for an exact, unreaped direct child with no other waitpid caller or Foundation reaper. Verify positive PID, expected generation, current UID, direct parent PID, start identity, executable and isolated process-group leadership. A PID or persisted/received ProcessIdentity alone is not adoption authority. The spawn owner must transfer exclusive wait/signal responsibility exactly once; failed adoption leaves cleanup with the original spawn owner.

The owner stores the original identity and a closed state: live/unreaped, terminal status, or ownership failure. One short mutex covers the final eligibility/identity check and signal syscall, every nonblocking waitpid call, and terminal-state publication. No copied identity may escape that critical section as later signal authority. Never hold it across blocking wait, sleep, pipe drainage, user callbacks, or publication to another lock domain. Separate immutable observation from mutation authority. A compile-time testing-only synchronization barrier may pause at the final-check/before-signal boundary to force the competing reap ordering. It must be a narrow, bounded, non-reentrant barrier: no user callback, ownership reentry, application-state/lifecycle lock acquisition or product I/O. Its implementation and routes must be absent from production binaries. This instrumentation exception does not permit a production callback under the mutex.

Only positive exact-child waitpid with WNOHANG is permitted. A successful terminal reap records the raw wait status once; repeated observers receive that same recorded result without another waitpid. EINTR is retryable without changing ownership. ECHILD or an unexpected result is an ownership failure, never a successful exit. After reap or ownership failure, every signal is refused without invoking kill. Identity mismatch or an unavailable identity query also refuses signaling and cannot be translated into successful cleanup.

Support only the fixed signals required by the callers. Group signaling requires the verified isolated group leader. Do not signal PID zero, a negative unvalidated identifier, a parent group or a borrowed process group. Unreaped direct-child ownership prevents the PID anchor from being recycled during a serialized signal; repeated pathname/start-time checks alone do not provide that property.

A bounded wait returns one of terminal, still owned/unresolved, or ownership failure. It does not discard ownership on timeout. A TERM/KILL escalation shares one monotonic budget with explicit post-KILL reap bounds. It must not clear tracking, close the last authority lease, or report cancellation complete simply to meet a wall-clock deadline. The responsible caller retains a cleanup owner and admission exclusion until verified terminal cleanup or explicit recovery. No detached, unowned cleanup task is allowed.

## Caller integration and complete correction boundary

WorkerClient transfers its spawned worker to this owner before exposing the session. WorkerSession stopping and all WorkerClient reap paths use that same instance. Preserve stop single-flight, stable outcome publication, child-drain and run-authority checks, takeover ordering and exactly-once callbacks. A failed spawn/admission is cleaned by the responsible owner before returning; do not publish an unowned child.

CancellationToken must retain a direct-child owner rather than an identity snapshot that can outlive its anchor. DescriptorProcessRunner and ClosedProcessRunner use that same owner for cancellation signals and waits. Preserve suspended publisher validation, closed start admission, descriptor/lease custody and all repository preflight checks. Do not weaken identity checks or replace a missing owner with raw kill.

Production launchctl/codesign operations must use an owned direct-child runner with an isolated group, bounded output and deadline, or another independently verified equivalent contract. Foundation's independent reaper cannot be protected by an app-local mutex; do not widen group signaling to accommodate nonleaders. Keep testing-only convenience callers classified separately.

The GitHub adapter requires a terminal post-KILL bound and retained unresolved ownership, while its current single-thread signal/reap safety remains intact. All affected paths need explicit cleanup evidence; an owner abstraction that is not wired into a caller does not fix that caller.

Recorded or orphaned processes are borrowed identities. They may support bounded observation but cannot be passed to the direct-child owner or signaled by a check-then-kill fallback. Complete automatic drainage after worker loss requires a supervisor retaining the unreaped child anchor under the signaling authority, or a separately verified atomic platform mechanism. Before implementing that custody transition, adopt its exact protocol, parent-death behavior, durable recovery, descriptor/lease topology and negative tests through a successor specification. A temporary refusal is containment only: it does not satisfy automatic child drainage or permit installed rollout. Keep this unresolved dependency visible until qualified; do not silently substitute manual cleanup for the approved product behavior.

## Verification

Use only disposable owned children and synthetic repositories. Do not force live PID reuse or inspect real protected targets. Deterministic testing seams pause after final identity validation but before the signal syscall while a competing observer attempts reap; prove the observer cannot release the anchor until signaling finishes. Also force reap-before-signal, two concurrent waiters, repeated terminal reads, EINTR, ECHILD, identity mismatch, unsupported signals, wrong-parent/UID/group adoption and zero timeout. Test retained unresolved ownership and failed cleanup without false success.

For every production integration, execute the real path with those orderings. Existing native fixture tests must exercise the production owner, not a separate stronger signal implementation. Keep all existing single-flight, takeover, worker-loss, authority, cancellation and report assertions. Add stable cases to compiled discovery and registration atomically; no filter or compile-only check substitutes for execution.

Build testing and production configurations. Production contains no injection seams or diagnostic-only routes. Bind source, binary, full expected/executed case roster, actual terminal statuses, cleanup and negative results. Rerun relevant runtime and native suites after each affected integration, followed by the full current candidate before publication. Local developer-toolchain results do not qualify release Xcode, minimum macOS, signatures, installation or physical UAT.

## Execution ownership and gates

The application engineer owns the core owner, WorkerSession/WorkerClient, token/runners and lifecycle callers. The release engineer owns case registration, evidence validation and hosted coverage. Independently review each bounded stage. OWNER-01 establishes and tests the shared owner; OWNER-02 integrates all direct-child callers and bounded cleanup; OWNER-03 resolves and adopts the complete borrowed-child custody design before its implementation; OWNER-04 executes full failure-path/runtime/native qualification and records delivery. These are ordered work packages under the parent plan, not independent completion claims for SF-05.

Rollback is a reviewed unpublished-candidate correction or normal source revert. Never restore an unsafe installed product to operation. Source publication, complete CI, signed release, installed migration/update, compatibility retirement and UAT remain separate gates. This specification changes no product UI or permissions and grants no live-installation authority beyond the existing accepted plan.

## Platform basis

Apple's [waitpid contract](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/System/Conceptual/ManPages_iPhoneOS/man2/wait.2.html) defines exact-child waits and WNOHANG. Verify the actual SDK, wait-status macros and process identity queries during native qualification. Apple XNU's [exit/reap implementation](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/bsd/kern/kern_exit.c) and [signal implementation](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/bsd/kern/kern_sig.c) inform the ownership model; moving source links alone do not establish the installed kernel's behavior. No unsupported private syscall or extra signing entitlement is authorized by this design.
